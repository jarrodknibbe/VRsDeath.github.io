// Call for Participation

Call for Contributions

Virtual Reality received a huge amount of research attention over the past decade. Our research community has examined novel hardware, collaboration, interaction techniques, visualisation, story-telling, and more. Recently, however, the future of VR has been less certain. Meta has laid off much of its workforce and whether we'll ever get another Quest headset remains unclear. Even flagship products like the Vision Pro, which straddle VR and XR, have struggled to find a solid foothold. Specialist, flagship devices like the Varjo remain available, but their future may be less certain if the overall appetite for VR wanes.

This workshop aims to ask: If VR fades away, how would we explain why through an HCI and interaction design lens? Are there generalisable lessons we could learn from its demise? Are there fundamental issues we must tackle to prevent the same fate for broader XR?

Submission Guidelines

  • Format: 1–2 page brief opinion/provocation paper (in the IEEE conference template).
  • Focus: A specific roadblock, inductive lesson, sensory limit, or critical critique of VR's trajectory[cite: 1].
  • Dissemination: These position pieces will not be published in the IEEE conference proceedings[cite: 1]. Instead, they will directly steer workshop discussions and be synthesized into a post-workshop Substack publication for the broader HCI/XR community[cite: 1].

// Interactive Format

No Keynotes. No Slide Decks.

Designed to be discursive, fast-paced, and highly interactive, the workshop convenes 20–30 researchers for intensive round-table affinity diagramming and debate[cite: 1]:

Hour 01
Surface the Roadblocks

Brainstorming fundamental stumbling blocks and rating their severity—from minor workable hitches to fatal interaction roadblocks[cite: 1].

Hour 02
Categorise & Generalise

Affinity diagramming roadblocks into universal interaction design claims, environmental requirements, and principles[cite: 1].

Hour 03
Cross-Technology Comparison

Testing distilled principles against historical and emerging technologies to see what refutes or reinforces our findings[cite: 1].

Hour 04
Synthesis & Report Draft

Capturing arguments into a shared living document and drafting our immediate public Substack report[cite: 1].
